Torque on an induction motor requires the rotor magnetic field to push against the stators magnetic field..
- if the two fields are aligned they push each other efficiently hence large torque
Thid relies on Phase differemce (angle btwn stator field and rotor current) and the rotor frequency.

At start up- Fr is very high 
	- Fr= sFs 
	- s a start up is near 1 , since rotor speed is low (near zero)
- This high frequency causes a high iinductive reactance in the rotor (X2)
		- X= 2 * pi * fL
- The High resistance causes rotor current to lag from the stator field

Appliying an external resistance pushes the current back into phase and into alignment with the stator current

### POWER FLOW IN AN INDUCTION MOTOR
	Input Power - Electrical per phase  = (sud3) Vline * Iline cos (theta) in a star system
	then some of the power is lost through the stator as 
	-Stator coppper losses
	-Stator Core losses
	The  Power that gets transferred to the rotot through the air Gap is called
		AIRGAP power
	Some of that power is lost as rotor copper losses and gets converted to Mechanical power
	The mechanical power is also depreciated by rotational losses to have useful output power

**Key parameters**
- Air gap power - given by Pin - Pstator loses
- It is related to inpu volatge, current and Load
- When load is increased, rotational speed decreases , increasing slip...This make the motor draw more power (line current) to meet the load needs
- Hence the increase in input power leads to increase in airgap power
